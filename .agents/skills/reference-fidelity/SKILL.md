# Reference Fidelity

## Purpose
Transfer selected editing techniques from a reference while adapting them to the user's footage and approved visual identity.

## Analyze observable evidence
Inspect the reference and record:
- scene structure and pacing;
- shot sizes and framing;
- transitions and scale changes;
- title and subtitle treatment;
- typography, alignment, color, spacing;
- graphic elements and motion;
- sound effects and their timing, when audio is available.

Separate confirmed observations from estimates. Do not invent exact fonts, timings, colors, or effects when they cannot be identified.

## Adapt, do not blindly copy
For each technique, label it:
- keep;
- adapt;
- exclude.

Explain why it suits or does not suit the source footage. Consider face position, headroom, gestures, background, orientation, resolution, and spoken content.

## Reference fidelity constraints
- Treat the user's selected reference features as requirements.
- Do not replace the reference's defining composition with generic templates.
- Do not add plates, outlines, shadows, gradients, stickers, or other design elements unless visible in the reference or explicitly approved.
- Do not assume subtitle styling from a generic preset.
- Do not infer spoken content from reference visuals.

## Deliverable
Create a concise reference analysis and timecoded edit plan tied to actual source moments. Mark uncertain observations. Include a short proof requirement for complex or technically uncertain effects.
